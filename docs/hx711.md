# HX711 bring-up

## Wiring

Target: Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-3.49 V2 (Rev1.1). Power off before wiring.

| HX711 module | Waveshare header label |
| --- | --- |
| VCC | 3V3, for a module supporting 3.3 V operation |
| GND | GND |
| DT / DOUT | IO44 |
| SCK / CLK | IO43 |

The GPIO numbers are not physical header positions. GPIO17/18 are the touch I²C bus on Rev1.1 and must not be used for HX711. GPIO43/44 are shared with UART0, so keep the configured USB Serial/JTAG console and do not attach a UART device. Do not feed 5 V logic to an ESP32 input. Modules with separate analog VCC and digital VDD need their own schematic checked; keep digital VDD at 3.3 V. The load cell connects to E+/E− and A+/A−; B is unused. Red E+, black E−, green A+, yellow A− is only a provisional color mapping for the user's four-wire sensor, not a universal standard. Confirm the cell's datasheet; yellow may instead be a shield.

Pin assignments live in `main/board349.hxx` and are exposed through `main/config.hxx`. The protocol is a dedicated clock/data interface, not I2C. Channel A gain 128 uses 24 data clocks plus one selection pulse. The module RATE pin still selects 10 or 80 SPS. A dedicated priority-5 task checks readiness every 1 ms (one RTOS tick), reads completed conversions, and timestamps them independently of the UI. A 128-entry queue bridges acquisition to the controller. The UI consumes batches approximately every 100 ms. This supports both rates without intentionally downsampling acquisition to the UI rate; actual timing still needs board validation.

## Calibration and weighing

1. Mount the cell correctly and keep the platform clear. Power the board via USB.
2. The initial display shows `Calibration required` once the sensor responds; it never treats ADC counts as grams.
3. Tap **Calibrate**, let the reading settle, and tap **Capture empty zero**.
4. Place an accurate 20 g, 50 g, or 100 g reference within the load cell's rated capacity. Wait for the reading to become Stable, then tap the corresponding mass. Never choose a nominal mass that differs from the actual reference.
5. Calibration saves to NVS. Remove the reference, close the dialog, and verify zero and at least one other known mass. Reboot and repeat the check. An explicit `Calibration not saved` message means the in-memory factor is usable only until reboot; repeat calibration after fixing storage.
6. For ingredient weighing, put a container on the platform, wait for **Stable**, then tap **Tare**. Tare does not rewrite the empty-platform reference in NVS.
7. For brewing, put the empty vessel (or prepared pour-over assembly) on the platform and set the dose. Tap **Start brew** before liquid arrives. Start captures a new tare and starts the timer; it does not detect a pour automatically. Finish records the result in RAM. Reset/Next waits for load removal and clears the temporary tare, not calibration.

Register equipment from the Object card. The reference step now captures a stable, calibrated gross measurement rather than the demo's 156.8 g constant. On the first stable placement, compare calibrated gross mass against registered empty weights with an inclusive ±40 g tolerance. A unique match selects the profile; multiple matches show **Choose object**, and no match remains unknown. Tap the Object card to select manually when needed. Explicit identity selection requires an available sensor and a positive load, but does not require the full drift stability window; tare/calibration/start remain stability-gated. Selection changes identity only, never tare or dose. Tap Tare with an empty portafilter before adding beans to measure a dose. Identity stays fixed until removal is confirmed by at least 300 ms of near-zero median readings; the algorithm does not reclassify while adding contents. Saving a profile retries an unresolved match on the next stable reading, so the object can stay on the platform during registration. Deleting a profile also retries unresolved/ambiguous matching. An already selected identity stays locked until removal. Live mode starts with no fictional equipment and shows **Register object** when no profiles exist. Profiles and brew history do not survive reboot. Register the empty object from the Object card after a reboot; calibration persistence does not imply profile persistence.

## Weight-change diagnostics

Run `make monitor /dev/ttyACM0` (substitute your board's serial port). INFO logs tagged `weight` appear when the main displayed weight changes, including the initial value and transitions to/from `?`. Unchanged values and screen redraws do not repeat the log. Weight-change lines are capped at one per second; rapid intermediate changes are coalesced to the latest visible value. The desktop live-test renderer emits the same diagnostic line to stdout; demo mode stays quiet.

Example format (illustrative values):

```text
I (...) weight: display=100.2 g raw=86773 sample_ms=123456 gross=100.2 g net=100.1612 g stable=0 ready=1 status=Settling...
```

`raw` is the latest signed ADC count, `sample_ms` its timestamp since boot, `gross` the calibrated filtered weight before tare, and `net` the filtered weight after tare at four decimal places (diagnostic precision, not claimed accuracy). `display` includes display hysteresis/deadband. `ready` indicates measurement availability; unavailable gross/net values are `NA`/`nan`. Raw and timestamp remain the last received sample on a disconnect, so a repeated timestamp with `ready=0` identifies stale data. A result screen can retain its completed weight independently of the current sensor reading.

Logging runs with UI refresh, never in the acquisition task or the clocking critical section. This logs visible changes rather than every ADC conversion. Capture about 10 seconds with the platform untouched and another 10 seconds with a fixed known mass to diagnose fluctuation.

A compact `hx711_diag` summary is emitted every five seconds, even if the weight label does not change. It is formatted under the controller lock and printed outside it. No per-sample logging is added.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `fail` | Bitmask: 1 = insufficient window/unavailable; 2 = standard deviation too high; 4 = drift too high; 8 = filtered value not settled. Values add, e.g. 6 = noise + drift. |
| `hold_ms` | Time continuously passing all checks; Stable requires 500 ms. `fail=0` with `stable=0` can mean confirmation is pending. |
| `sd=value/limit`, `drift=value/limit` | Current noise standard deviation and signed slope, with their limits. |
| `residual`, `p2p` | Filter-to-window-mean difference and window peak-to-peak variation; based on median-filtered samples. Residual uses the SD limit. |
| `n`, `span_ms`, `drift_n`, `drift_span_ms` | Samples and duration in the short noise/settling window and separate long drift window. Short-window statistics are not populated until sufficient short history exists; `fail=1` also covers insufficient drift history. |
| `units` | Grams and grams/second after calibration; counts and counts/second beforehand. |
| `hz` | Samples consumed per second over the diagnostic interval, not a direct electrical RATE-pin measurement. |
| `age_ms`, `max_age_ms`, `batch` | Latest sample age, maximum queue delivery age, and largest batch consumed in the reporting interval. Ages use the same `esp_timer` clock; do not subtract the ESP-IDF log prefix from `sample_ms` to estimate latency. |
| `overflow`, `faults` | Cumulative acquisition queue overflows and measurement invalidations/saturations. |
| `profiles`, `object` | Number of registered profiles and matching state: `no_profiles`, `sensor_unavailable`, `waiting_stable`, `empty`, `pending`, `no_match`, `ambiguous`, `matched`, or `session_locked`. No extra log line is added. |
| `factor`, `zero` | Current calibration counts/g and empty-platform raw zero, useful for detecting an incorrect reference mass or calibration. |

Capture six consecutive `hx711_diag` lines (about 30 seconds) with the load untouched. `fail` tells us which criterion is preventing stability; timing and calibration fields help distinguish measurement variation from stale data or an incorrect scale factor. These diagnostics do not prove a particular hardware cause by themselves.

## Stable weight display

The main live weight label uses 0.15 g hysteresis and a ±0.15 g zero deadband around the calibrated/tared zero. For example, a held 100.0 g stays unchanged while readings alternate between 99.9 and 100.1 g. A change to 100.16 g updates the display to 100.2 g. Slow additions accumulate against the held value rather than being discarded sample by sample. Invalid readings immediately show `?`; tare, calibration, and phase changes reset the display state. Filtering does not alter raw counts, calibrated weight, flow, recorded results, recognition, or captured equipment references.

## Corner Cases & Failure Modes

- Two profiles whose ±40 g bands overlap can both match: selection stays ambiguous rather than picking the closest. For example, 150 g and 200 g profiles both match 175 g. A filled object can also fall in another object's band; weight alone cannot distinguish that case. The 40 g tolerance is never used to infer coffee dose or automatically subtract an empty weight.
- DOUT high or disconnected: no busy wait; after 1 second without a sample, show `?` and disable tare/start. An input pull-up handles an unconnected DOUT. A DOUT stuck low is rejected if it does not release after the 25th pulse.
- ADC rail values or calibrated magnitude above the software display guard of 10,000 g: show overload/unavailable. This software guard is **not** the cell's safe mechanical capacity. Do not overload your cell.
- Reverse polarity: signed 24-bit decoding and a negative calibration factor are supported.
- Noise and filter-settling checks require at least eight median-filtered samples spanning at least 1.0 s within a trailing 1.2 s window. Drift regression uses a separate trailing 3.2 s window with at least 3.0 s of history. After calibration, standard deviation must be at most 0.10 g (minimum 2 raw counts), and absolute regression slope at most 0.05 g/s (minimum 1 count/s). Before calibration the limits are 150 counts and 50 counts/s. The low-pass reading must also be within the standard-deviation limit of the window mean. All checks must pass continuously for a further 500 ms before Stable is reported; any failure clears Stable immediately. Tare, calibration, registration, and Start use this gate. These configurable starting thresholds are not an accuracy guarantee.
- Calibration rejects zero/negative/nonfinite reference masses and changes below 1000 counts. A disconnect cancels a captured zero. Closing the dialog cancels an unfinished calibration without changing the saved factor.
- During brewing, sensor failure or a net decrease greater than 5 g in one update latches uncertainty. Stable gross mass at or below 2 g is treated as removal. Slow/partial removal is not guaranteed to be detected; inspect the result and reset after disturbance. No automatic restart occurs on reconnect.
- A median of three rejects isolated spikes; a time-based exponential low-pass filter (tau = 0.3 s) follows real increases and decreases. A step reaches about 95% after 0.9 s plus the median delay. There is no automatic zero tracking or positive-only accumulation. Flow uses acquisition timestamps rather than UI-loop timing. It is a filtered weight derivative, not a calibrated volumetric flow measurement.
- Stability statistics use samples before low-pass smoothing, so smoothing does not hide drift. A longer drift window reduces sensitivity to small oscillations that otherwise look like a slope over 1.2 s. The 0.05 g/s drift and 0.10 g noise limits are unchanged. Recognition uses the stable window mean to avoid a filter-lag bias at the ±40 g matching boundary.
- Saturation, a >1 s sample gap, or a queue overflow resets filter/stability history. Overflow discards the queued backlog and explicitly marks the next reading as discontinuous. Stale queued readings are rejected. During a brew these faults latch uncertainty; no silent continuation occurs. Duplicate or out-of-order sample timestamps are ignored. Reconnection requires three fresh samples before a weight is available and fresh drift history plus a 500 ms confirmation interval before tare/start (roughly 3.5–4 s from fresh samples).
- NVS failures are reported, not “repaired” by erasing the partition. Calibration uses one versioned blob and one commit; temporary tare is never persisted. Recalibrate if the cell, gain, supply, mechanics, or wiring changes.

## Adaptation and validation

Adapted from user-supplied `smart_scale.bak(1).tgz`: `main/hx711.hxx` and `main/scale.cxx` supplied the signed conversion, gain-selection, factor, and separate tare/reference concepts. The old GPIO14/32 assignment, fixed `{866, -78000}` calibration, task delays inside critical sections, unbounded ready loop, and old OLED stack were not imported.

`make test` runs the existing session suite plus signed conversion, negative calibration, tare versus gross, invalid calibration, timeout/reconnect, saturation, sensor removal, and live-session tests. `make preview` also exercises touch calibration, tare, Start, and sensor failure through the actual LVGL renderer using injected raw readings.

Before relying on weight: verify SCK timing with a logic analyzer, calibrate and test multiple reference masses across the working range, repeat tare/reboot, unplug the HX711 during a brew, and verify touch responsiveness and load-removal behavior on the actual board. No physical board is attached to automated tests.
